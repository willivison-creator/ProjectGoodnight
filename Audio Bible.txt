
# GOODNIGHT, JACKSON
## Audio Production Bible
### Version 1.0

---

# Purpose:

The audio should feel less like a song and less like a traditional newscast.

It should feel like an evening conversation that just happens to be musical.

The listener should immediately recognize the first few seconds as "Goodnight, Jackson."

Every episode should sound like it belongs to the same series.

The news changes. 

The identity never does.

---

# Overall Audio Description:

Country rock and acoustic folk-pop with spoken word and melodic male vocals and a driving backbeat. Acoustic guitar plays steady eighth-note strumming patterns. Clean electric guitar plays rhythmic eighth-note strumming patterns while a second electric guitar provides melodic fills and arpeggios with light overdrive and bridge-pickup twang. Electric bass follows the root notes with a warm, rounded tone. The bass guitar follows the kick drum with a steady root-note pulse. Drums feature a light snare backbeat with a short decay and a tight kick drum. A male vocalist delivers a spoken-word style narration in the intro before transitioning into a melodic baritone delivery. Spoken word narration transitions into sung vocal harmonies in the choruses.  The tempo is 105 to 110 BPM, 4/4 time signature, in the key of G major.


Example Flow:

[Intro]
[acoustic guitar strumming, tight percussion]

[Verse 1]
[spoken word male vocals]

[Chorus]
[sung male vocals with harmony, bass and electric guitar enter, full band arrangement]
[melodic baritone vocals]

[Verse 2]
[spoken word male vocals, acoustic guitar and light drums continue]

[Chorus]
[melodic baritone vocals, bass and electric guitar re-enter, full band arrangement]

Etc.

[Outro]
[sung male vocals, full band arrangement]

# Runtime:

Target Runtime: 3:30–4:30

Minimum: 3:00

Maximum: 5:00

The pace should never feel rushed.
Listeners should feel invited into the broadcast.

---

# Musical Personality:

Overall Mood:
• Warm • Familiar • Hopeful • Relaxed • Neighborly • Confident • Calm • Optimistic

Avoid:
• Aggressive • Dramatic • Overly emotional • Sad • Triumphant • Comedic • Over-produced

The emotional goal is to help listeners unwind while staying informed.

---

# Musical Style:

Country-pop with modern broadcast polish.

Imagine the warmth of a hometown radio station combined with contemporary Nashville production.

Instrumentation should support the storytelling rather than compete with it.

Primary instruments:
• Acoustic guitar • Clean electric guitar • Electric bass • Live drum kit • Piano or warm keyboard pad • Light percussion only when needed

No heavy rock guitars.
No electronic dance elements.
No light, classical piano.
No cinematic scoring.

The music should create momentum without demanding attention.

---

# Tempo:

Target: 112–116 BPM
Steady 4/4.

Consistent throughout the episode.
No dramatic tempo changes.

# lEAD VOCAL:

The lead voice is the anchor of the show.
He is not "performing."
He is communicating.
His role is a narrator.

Characteristics:
• Warm • Mature • Friendly • Calm • Trustworthy • Slight smile in the voice • Excellent diction • Natural pacing • Never rushed

The delivery should be speaking and narration.
Think storytelling rather than melodic performance.
The listener should feel as though someone is sitting beside them at the kitchen table explaining what happened today.

Insert natural pauses.

Examples:
• After greetings.
• After significant news.
• Before weather.

Emphasize:
• Community names
• Important locations
• Major developments
• Positive achievements

Do not over-emphasize statistics or numbers.

# HARMONY VOCALS:

The backing vocals are the signature of the show.

They are not decoration.

They are a second narrator.

Their job is to reinforce the emotional rhythm of the broadcast.

They operate as the chorus.

They provide the transition between topics.

They sing with musical accompaniment.

They provide harmony when the music volume increases.

## Characteristics:

• Three male harmony voices • Rich but understated • Tight harmony • Wide stereo placement
• Accompanied by louder music • Soft entrances • Gentle exits

## Harmony Direction:

Backing vocals provide the harmony between pauses by the lead.

Backing vocals are used to transition between topics.

Backing vocals begin or conclude stories and topics.

Backing vocals provide rhythmic storytelling that support the lead.

They never overpower the lead.

They never sing at the same time as the lead.

They never compete.

Instead they:

• Transition between topics supported by musical accompaniment.
• Echo important phrases.
• Harmonize additional facts.
• Reinforce emotional transitions and key words.
• Support the closing.
• Celebrate the positive story.
• Create emotional lift • Smooth transitions

# Vocal Interaction:

The lead always owns the story.

The harmonies support the story.

Example interaction:
Lead: "Meanwhile here in Mississippi..."
Harmony: "...right here at home..."

Lead: "The community came together..."
Harmony: "...just like always..."

The backup voices should sound supportive rather than interruptive.

---

# Pacing:

Every story follows the same rhythm:
• Introduce
• Explain
• Transition
• Resolve
• Pause
• Move on.

Speak naturally.

Average pace:
145–165 words per minute.

Allow brief pauses after major stories.

Natural pauses are critical.

Silence is part of the production.

Never rush transitions.

Never sound like headlines are being read from a teleprompter.

---

# Transitions:

Transitions should feel effortless.

Examples:
"Meanwhile..."
"Closer to home..."
"Across the state..."
"On the sports front..."
"And finally..."

Never use abrupt topic changes.

Avoid sudden changes in vocal intensity.

The audience should feel guided rather than redirected.

---

# Music Dynamics:

Music should gently rise beneath important stories narrated by lead vocals.

Lower slightly during factual reporting.

Lift during positive stories.

Become warmest during the closing.

Never overwhelm the narration.

---

# Mixing:

Lead vocal: Always centered.

Harmony vocals: Wide stereo image.

Acoustic guitar: Wide.

Bass: Centered.

Kick: Centered.

Snare: Centered.

Pads: Wide but subtle.

Reverb: Warm and natural.

Compression: Broadcast-ready without sounding over-processed.

# Episode Structure:

• Opening
• Welcome.
• Set the mood.
• National Headlines
• Global News
• Sports
• Mississippi News
• Jackson Area News
• Weather
• Positive Community Story
• Closing Reflection
• Signature Sign-off

This order should remain constant across every episode.

---

# Weather:

Relax slightly.

Weather is conversational.

Imagine discussing tomorrow with a neighbor.

---

# Positive Story:

Smile can be heard in the voice.

Do not exaggerate.

Allow warmth to develop naturally.

---

# Ending:

Every broadcast should slow slightly during the final twenty seconds.

The listener should feel the day gently coming to an end.

The final words should feel familiar and comforting.

The emotional takeaway is:
"The day is over.
You're informed.
Tomorrow will take care of itself."

Leave comfortable pauses.

Allow the final words to settle.

The sign-off should become a recognizable signature.

# Identity Rules:

Maintain:
• Warm narration
• Neighborly tone
• Consistent pacing
• Gentle transitions
• Balanced dynamics
• Natural breathing
• Country-pop influence
• Warm acoustic instrumentation
• Broadcast polish
• Subtle harmony
• Natural vocal delivery
• Even pacing

Relaxed ending

The show should never chase viral trends.

Never sound sensational.

Never manufacture excitement.

The confidence comes from consistency.

Listeners should return because the experience is dependable.

The goal is not simply to report the news.

The goal is to become part of someone's nightly routine.

---

# Success Criteria:

A first-time listener should think:
"That was enjoyable."

A regular listener should think:
"It feels good to be home."

If an episode can be identified as "Goodnight, Jackson" within the first ten seconds without hearing the title, the production has succeeded.
