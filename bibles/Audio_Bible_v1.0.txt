# PROJECT GOODNIGHT

## Audio Production Bible

### Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

The audio should feel less like a song and less like a traditional newscast.

It should feel like an evening conversation that just happens to be musical.

The listener should immediately recognize the first few seconds as "Goodnight, Jackson."

Every episode should sound like it belongs to the same series.

The news changes.

The identity never does.

---

# Runtime

Target Runtime:
3:30–4:30

Minimum:
3:00

Maximum:
5:00

The pace should never feel rushed.

Listeners should feel invited into the broadcast.

---

# Musical Personality

Overall Mood

• Warm
• Familiar
• Hopeful
• Relaxed
• Neighborly
• Confident
• Calm
• Optimistic

Avoid

• Aggressive
• Dramatic
• Overly emotional
• Sad
• Triumphant
• Comedic
• Over-produced

The emotional goal is to help listeners unwind while staying informed.

---

# Musical Style

Country-pop with modern broadcast polish.

Imagine the warmth of a hometown radio station combined with contemporary Nashville production.

Instrumentation should support the storytelling rather than compete with it.

Primary instruments:

• Acoustic guitar
• Clean electric guitar
• Electric bass
• Live drum kit
• Piano or warm keyboard pad
• Light percussion only when needed

No heavy rock guitars.

No electronic dance elements.

No cinematic scoring.

The music should create momentum without demanding attention.

---

# Tempo

Target:
112–116 BPM

Steady 4/4.

Consistent throughout the episode.

No dramatic tempo changes.

---

# Lead Vocal

The lead voice is the anchor of the show.

He is not "performing."

He is communicating.

Characteristics:

• Warm
• Mature
• Friendly
• Calm
• Trustworthy
• Slight smile in the voice
• Excellent diction
• Natural pacing
• Never rushed

The delivery should live between speaking and singing.

Think rhythmic storytelling rather than melodic performance.

The listener should feel as though someone is sitting beside them at the kitchen table explaining what happened today.

---

# Harmony Vocals

The backing vocals are the signature of the show.

They are not decoration.

They are a second narrator.

Their job is to reinforce the emotional rhythm of the broadcast.

Characteristics:

• Three male harmony voices
• Rich but understated
• Tight harmony
• Wide stereo placement
• Soft entrances
• Gentle exits

They never overpower the lead.

Instead they:

• Echo important phrases
• Finish thoughts
• Reinforce key words
• Create emotional lift
• Smooth transitions

The harmonies should feel almost conversational.

---

# Vocal Interaction

The lead always owns the story.

The harmonies support the story.

Example interaction:

Lead:
"Meanwhile here in Mississippi..."

Harmony:
"...right here at home..."

Lead:
"The community came together..."

Harmony:
"...just like always..."

The backup voices should sound supportive rather than interruptive.

---

# Pacing

Every story follows the same rhythm.

Introduce

Explain

Transition

Resolve

Pause

Move on.

Natural pauses are critical.

Silence is part of the production.

---

# Story Transitions

Transitions should feel effortless.

Examples:

"Meanwhile..."

"Closer to home..."

"Across the state..."

"On the sports front..."

"And finally..."

Never use abrupt topic changes.

---

# Music Dynamics

Music should gently rise beneath important stories.

Lower slightly during factual reporting.

Lift during positive stories.

Become warmest during the closing.

Never overwhelm the narration.

---

# Mixing

Lead vocal:
Always centered.

Harmony vocals:
Wide stereo image.

Acoustic guitar:
Wide.

Bass:
Centered.

Kick:
Centered.

Snare:
Centered.

Pads:
Wide but subtle.

Reverb:
Warm and natural.

Compression:
Broadcast-ready without sounding over-processed.

---

# Episode Structure

Opening

Welcome.

Set the mood.

National Headlines

Global News

Sports

Mississippi News

Jackson Area News

Weather

Positive Community Story

Closing Reflection

Signature Sign-off

This order should remain constant across every episode.

---

# Ending

Every broadcast should slow slightly during the final twenty seconds.

The listener should feel the day gently coming to an end.

The final words should feel familiar and comforting.

The emotional takeaway is:

"The day is over.

You're informed.

Tomorrow will take care of itself."

---

# Identity Rules

The show should never chase viral trends.

Never sound sensational.

Never manufacture excitement.

The confidence comes from consistency.

Listeners should return because the experience is dependable.

The goal is not simply to report the news.

The goal is to become part of someone's nightly routine.

---

# Success Criteria

A first-time listener should think:

"That was enjoyable."

A regular listener should think:

"It feels good to be home."

If an episode can be identified as "Goodnight, Jackson" within the first ten seconds without hearing the title, the production has succeeded.

---

Version 1.0

This document is intended to remain stable over time. Changes should be deliberate, documented, and versioned so the show's identity evolves without losing the qualities that make it recognizable.
