# PROJECT GOODNIGHT

## Editorial Bible

### Version 1.0

---

# Purpose

The Editorial Bible defines how every episode selects, prioritizes, summarizes, and presents news.

Its purpose is to ensure that every episode feels consistent regardless of what stories occurred that day.

The audience should never feel overwhelmed, manipulated, or exhausted.

The editorial mission is to help listeners understand the day—not relive its chaos.

---

# Editorial Principles

Every editorial decision should support these principles.

## Accuracy

Facts take priority over speed.

Never knowingly report false or unverified information.

If a developing story cannot be confirmed from reliable reporting, it should either be omitted or clearly described as developing.

---

## Clarity

Explain stories in everyday language.

Avoid unnecessary jargon.

If a technical term must be used, briefly explain it.

---

## Relevance

Choose stories because they matter to listeners—not because they generated the most online engagement.

Ask:

* Does this affect people's lives?
* Is this nationally or locally significant?
* Would a reasonable listener expect to hear about it?

---

## Balance

A typical episode should include a mix of:

* National news
* International news
* Sports
* Mississippi news
* Jackson-area news
* Weather
* One uplifting community story

No single category should dominate unless justified by an extraordinary event.

---

## Calm

Goodnight, Jackson is an evening broadcast.

The goal is to leave listeners informed and settled.

Avoid sensational language.

Avoid speculation.

Avoid emotional escalation.

---

# Editorial Workflow

Every episode follows the same workflow.

## Stage 1 — Collect

Gather candidate stories from trusted sources.

Separate them into categories.

National

International

Sports

State

Local

Weather

Community

---

## Stage 2 — Verify

Before a story is included:

* Confirm it has been reported by a reliable source.
* Check that names, dates, and locations are correct.
* Avoid repeating rumors or social media speculation.

---

## Stage 3 — Rank

Each candidate story receives a priority score.

Suggested criteria:

* Public importance
* Geographic relevance
* Timeliness
* Listener interest
* Lasting significance

Stories are ranked before scripting begins.

---

## Stage 4 — Select

Typical episode:

National: 2–3 stories

World: 1–2 stories

Sports: 2 stories

Mississippi: 1–2 stories

Jackson: 1–2 stories

Weather: 1 forecast

Positive story: 1 feature

Quality matters more than quantity.

---

# Story Structure

Every story should answer four questions.

What happened?

Why does it matter?

Who is affected?

What comes next?

If these questions cannot be answered concisely, simplify the story.

---

# Story Length

Target:

20–35 seconds per story.

Never exceed one minute unless the event is of exceptional importance.

Listeners should always feel the show is moving forward.

---

# Writing Style

Write for the ear.

Not for the eye.

Preferred:

"Today..."

"This afternoon..."

"Meanwhile..."

"Closer to home..."

Avoid:

"According to..."

"It has been reported that..."

Unless attribution is necessary for clarity.

---

# Attribution

Use attribution when:

* Facts are disputed.
* Information comes from official statements.
* Statistics are quoted.
* Claims cannot be independently verified.

Examples:

"The National Weather Service forecasts..."

"State officials announced..."

---

# Sensitive Topics

Some stories require additional care.

Examples include:

* Natural disasters
* Fatal incidents
* Public health
* Crime
* Children
* Military conflict

Guidelines:

Report respectfully.

Avoid graphic descriptions.

Do not speculate.

Do not dramatize.

End with factual information or available resources when appropriate.

---

# Politics

Political stories should be reported based on public significance, not partisan appeal.

Avoid emotionally charged adjectives.

Describe actions and decisions rather than assigning motives.

---

# Crime

Only include crime stories when they are:

* Significant to public safety
* Major regional events
* Necessary for awareness

Avoid treating routine crime reports as entertainment.

---

# Weather

Weather should sound conversational.

Begin with the overall feel of tomorrow.

Then mention temperatures, precipitation, or severe weather if relevant.

Example:

"Tomorrow looks warm and humid, with afternoon highs near ninety-two and a chance of scattered thunderstorms after lunch."

---

# Sports

Sports should celebrate competition without assuming every listener follows every league.

Prioritize:

1. Mississippi teams
2. SEC
3. Major national stories
4. International events when especially significant

---

# Positive Story

Every episode ends with hope.

Suitable topics include:

Community volunteers

Schools

Healthcare

Small businesses

Historic preservation

Festivals

Scientific achievements

Acts of kindness

Environmental restoration

The final story should remind listeners that good things continue to happen.

---

# Story Transitions

Use natural transitions.

Examples:

"Meanwhile..."

"Turning to sports..."

"Back here at home..."

"And finally..."

Avoid abrupt changes.

---

# Episode Review

Before publication, confirm:

✔ Facts verified

✔ Story order followed

✔ Runtime within target

✔ Positive ending included

✔ No sensational language

✔ Tone remains calm and conversational

---

# Editorial Integrity

Goodnight, Jackson exists to inform.

Not persuade.

Not entertain through outrage.

Not maximize engagement.

Trust is earned one episode at a time.

Every editorial decision should protect that trust.

---

# Version Control

This Editorial Bible is the governing document for all future script generation.

Changes should be versioned and documented.

The objective is steady improvement without changing the core identity of the show.
