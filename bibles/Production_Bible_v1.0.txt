# PROJECT GOODNIGHT

## Production Bible

### Version 1.0

---

# Mission

"Goodnight, Jackson" is a nightly three-to-five minute audio broadcast that helps people end the day feeling informed, grounded, and optimistic.

The show is not designed to maximize clicks or outrage.

It is designed to answer one question:
"What happened today, and what should I know before tomorrow?"

Listeners should finish every episode feeling calmer than when they started.

---

# Audience

The ideal listener is someone relaxing after dinner, driving home from work, or getting ready for bed.

The audience values:

• trustworthy reporting
• local relevance
• sports
• weather
• positive community stories
• a familiar nightly routine

---

# Emotional Arc
Every episode should follow this emotional progression:
Beginning
Curious
↓
Middle
Informed
↓
Ending
Hopeful
The ending should always leave the listener smiling.
Never anxious.

---

# Runtime

Target:
3 minutes 30 seconds

Acceptable:
3–5 minutes

Never exceed six minutes.

---

# Story Order

This order NEVER changes.

1. Welcome
2. National headlines
3. World headlines
4. Sports
5. Mississippi news
6. Jackson-area news
7. Weather
8. Positive community story
9. Closing

Consistency builds familiarity.

---

# Editorial Rules

Every story should answer three questions:

What happened?

Why does it matter?

What happens next?

Never read headlines without context.

---

# Writing Style

Imagine the best local TV anchor you've ever watched.

Sentences should sound spoken—not written.

Bad:
"The Department announced..."

Better:
"Today, the Department announced..."

Avoid:
• clickbait
• dramatic adjectives
• political opinions
• sarcasm
• fear

Use:
• conversational language
• contractions
• warmth
• confidence

---

# Tone

Think:
"I'm your neighbor telling you what happened today."

Not:
"I'm a newsreader."

---

# Humor

Small smiles.

Never jokes.

Never comedy.

A light observation is fine.

For example:
"Looks like Mississippi remembered it's July."

Perfect.

---

# Weather

Never read raw numbers first.

Instead:

"Another hot Mississippi afternoon is ahead tomorrow..."

Then mention temperatures.

---

# Sports

Prioritize:
National
SEC
Ole Miss
Rival Mississippi State 
Chicago Cubs
New Orleans Saints
Professional teams with Mississippi connections

---

# Positive Story

Every episode ends with something uplifting.

Examples:

• local business opening
• school achievement
• charity
• volunteer effort
• hospital success
• rescue
• festival
• historical anniversary
• hometown hero

This section is mandatory.

---

# Closing

Every episode ends slowly.

No rushing.

Example rhythm:
"That's today's story.
Thanks for spending a few minutes with us.
Take care of one another.
Sleep well...
and goodnight, Jackson."

---

# Things We Never Do

Never yell.
Never argue.
Never debate.
Never sensationalize.
Never leave the audience worried.

---

# Success Metric
If someone listened every night for a month...
They should begin recognizing the show's rhythm before the words.
That familiarity is the product.
